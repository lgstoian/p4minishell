ESP32

ESP32 is a family of low-cost, energy-efficient microcontrollers that support both Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. The chips are designed by Espressif Systems and combine a Tensilica Xtensa or RISC-V processor with wireless radios, SRAM and peripheral controllers on one die.

The ESP32 was announced in September 2016 as the successor to the ESP8266. It is manufactured by TSMC on a 40 nm process and is typically embedded on device-specific printed circuit boards or offered as part of development kits with GPIO headers.

Features

The original ESP32 integrates a dual-core Tensilica Xtensa LX6 at up to 240 MHz, 520 KiB of SRAM, flash and PSRAM interfaces, and an 802.11 b/g/n Wi-Fi radio with Bluetooth 4.2 BR/EDR and LE. Later family members add Xtensa LX7 cores, RISC-V cores, USB OTG, more GPIOs and additional peripherals. All variants share the same SDK and are largely code-compatible.

QFN packaged chip and module

ESP32 silicon is housed in quad-flat no-leads (QFN) packages of varying sizes. The ESP-WROOM-32 module combines the chip with a crystal oscillator, flash memory, filter capacitors and an inverted-F PCB antenna.

The package exposes 48 connection pads along the sides and one large thermal pad on the bottom. Module boards add the antenna, shielding and decoupling that a bare chip needs.

Chips and modules

Since the original release, a number of variants have been introduced: the ESP32-S2, ESP32-S3, ESP32-C2, ESP32-C3, ESP32-C5, ESP32-C6, ESP32-H2 and the ESP32-P4. The ESP32-P4 is a high-performance dual-core RISC-V part aimed at image and voice processing, while the ESP32-C6 adds Wi-Fi 6 and 802.15.4 for Thread and Zigbee.

A die shot shows how much of the silicon area is covered by the power distribution network on the top metal layers. The radio and digital logic share the same package but are laid out as separate blocks.

Programming

Start with the official ESP-IDF page, then pick a language binding or framework.

Programming languages, frameworks and environments used with the ESP32 include ESP-IDF, the Arduino core, MicroPython and CircuitPython, Espruino, Mongoose OS, Rust, Swift, Zig and Forth. Most toolchains talk to the chip over USB-UART or the built-in USB Serial/JTAG peripheral.

  • ESP-IDF - Espressif's official IoT Development Framework
  • Arduino-ESP32 - the Arduino core for ESP32, S2, S3 and C3
  • MicroPython and CircuitPython - Python 3 for microcontrollers
  • Espruino - a JavaScript SDK and firmware
  • Rust and Zig - systems languages with growing embedded support

Reception and use

Commercial, industrial and academic products use the ESP32 for connected sensors, lighting, wearables and audio. Examples include Alibaba's IoT LED wristbands, DingTalk's biometric attendance terminal and a long list of hobbyist development boards such as the ESP32-DevKitC and NodeMCU-32S.

The chip is popular because it pairs Wi-Fi and Bluetooth with enough RAM and flash for a small TCP/IP stack and an application, at a low unit price. Its successor families keep the same programming model while adding security features, more memory and newer radios.
